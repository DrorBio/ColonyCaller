Recommended: full words, ideally the NATO phonetic alphabet

Speech recognition is most reliable with full, distinct words. Single letters like “G” are short and many of them rhyme (B, C, D, E, G, P, T, V), so they get misheard more often. If a letter keeps being missed, say a word instead. Colony names work well, and NATO words were designed to sound distinct over noisy radio. Words marked with a check are already in use.

    Voice commands

    Say a colony’s name or any of its other words. Plurals work too. The tiles show the words for each type.

    granulocyte
    Counts one CFU-G (so do “G” and “golf”). CFU-Eo is “echo” and CFU-E is “yankee”, since the speech model doesn’t know the colony names.
    granulocyte macrophage
    Counts one CFU-GM. Say it in one breath, or say “GM”
    macrophage macrophage burst
    Counts three at once
    mike mike mike mike
    Counts four. With the on-device engine, repeats are just repeats.
    undo three
    Removes the last three counts (a number also works with a call word: “four mikes”)
    tally
    Adds to a general tally, for counting anything (so does “alpha”)
    undo / scratch
    Removes the last count
    next plate
    Starts a new plate
